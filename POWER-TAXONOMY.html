0. How to read this

The goal is a single power number per unit/loadout/wave so we can say “a wave needs to be ≈ X power” and then pick a composition (skulk + lerk + carapace…) that lands there. Before any such number is trustworthy, three things must be true, and they shape every table below:

  • Every stat is cited to shipped Lua, not memory. This project has been burned repeatedly by facts that were true of an older build (e.g. Vector:GetRangeTo does not exist in 344). Each row carries a file:line.
  • “Damage” and “health” are not scalars. NS2 damage has a type and the defender has armor that absorbs by type. The same bite does very different work against a rifle marine and an L3+carapace marine. So power is matchup-dependent — §1 makes that precise, and it is the central difficulty of the whole exercise.
  • Power is also time- and economy-dependent. A marine with 90 research done is a different animal than one with 0, and when they get there is set by cost + build/research time + income. So a wave’s power must be read against the marine state at that wave, not a fixed reference. §3.2 (biomass) and §2.5 (research) are the clocks.

1. The damage & armor model (the foundation for any power number)

Damage lands through one pipeline: DamageTypes.lua. Armor is a second health pool that absorbs a fraction of incoming damage at a per-type rate. To compare units we must convert everything to effective health (EHP) and effective DPS against a stated target.

EHP vs a given damage type = health + armor × (absorption-rate of that type)
  e.g. L2 marine (100 hp / 70 armor) vs Normal: EHP = 100 + 70×2 = 240; vs Gas: EHP = 100 + 70×0 = 100; vs Heavy: 100 + 70×1 = 170.
DPS = damage-per-hit × hits-per-second, then × the type multiplier against the target class. Hit rate is the weak link: several alien attacks and the rifle/HMG/minigun cadences are animation-driven (C++/asset), not in Lua — see caveat.
Consequence for the power stat: a unit’s power is not one number, it is a row of a matchup matrix (unit × opposing-state → effective output). Any single scalar must be taken against a named reference state. §5 builds on this.

4. The engine’s own power prior: PointValue

NS2 already ships a per-entity worth number — kTechDataPointValue, summed by PointGiverMixin:GetPointValue() (used for kill/build score). It is UWE’s own implicit balance weighting, so it is a legitimate starting prior for our power stat:

Unit / structurePVReading
Skulk / Gorge / Lerk / Fade / Onos5 / 7 / 15 / 20 / 30the lifeform ladder is ~6× from chaff to tank
Rifle marine / Jetpack / Exosuit5 / 10 / 20a marine is worth a skulk; an exo is worth an Onos
Hive / Command Station30 / 20aliens value their command structure higher
Weapons (shotgun 10 · HMG 15 · rail/minigun 10 · mines 2)—the buy-cost ladder roughly tracks it
Why PV alone is not enough: it is a static kill-score, blind to the matchup (an Onos is “30” whether it faces a rifle marine or an exo with railguns), to sustain, and to the fact that our horde has no economy. PV is a great shape prior — we should sanity-check any power formula against it — but not the answer.

5. Toward a single power stat (the brainstorm)

5.1 What one number has to survive

Any scalar we pick must not lose these five things, or it will mis-rank waves:

  1. Type effectiveness — fade stab (Structural) vs an armor-heavy marine ≠ vs a structure; spores (Gas) ignore armor entirely. → damage must be effective vs a stated defender.
  2. Durability vs the same threat — EHP, not HP. Onos 700/450 is 1600 EHP vs Normal but only ~700 vs Gas.
  3. Rate — DPS, not per-hit. The asset-cooldown gap (§3.5) bites here.
  4. Reachability — range/mobility decides whether a unit can engage at all (a melee unit vs a kiting exo contributes ~0). Lerk flight, skulk leap, jetpack.
  5. Sustain & numbers — a renewable marine vs a spent alien; a wave of 15 chaff vs 1 tank of equal PV.

5.2 The honest shape: power is a row, not a number

Because of (1)-(2), a unit’s true contribution is a function of who it is fighting. So we define power relative to a reference state — the marine loadout we expect at wave N — and get a scalar that is honest as long as we name the reference. Concretely, for unit u against marine-state M:

Offense(u→M) = effective DPS of u vs M’s EHP
Defense(u←M) = M’s time-to-kill u = u’s EHP-vs-M-DPS ÷ M’s DPS
Strength(u vs M) = Offense × Defense  (the classic Lanchester “worth” — a unit that kills twice as fast and dies twice as slowly is 4× the strength)

Wave power = the Lanchester-corrected sum of its units’ strengths (not a flat sum — concentrated fire beats equal numbers, which is exactly why “15 skulks” and “1 onos” must not score the same unless we say so).

5.4 A concrete first cut to react to

Define a Threat Index (TI) per unit against the expected marine state at that wave, then size a wave to a target TI:

TI(u, N) = [ dpsₙ(u) × ehpₙ(u) ]½ × reach(u) × sustain(u)
geometric mean of offense & defense (so neither alone dominates); reach,sustain are 0.5–1.5 multipliers; everything normalized so a base rifle marine = TI 1.0

Wave threat = Σ TI(u) over the wave, optionally ÷ (number of marines) to keep it per-player.
Wave design target: match the marine team’s TI-at-wave-N × a difficulty band (DESIGN §4 says 1.0–1.25).

Worked sketch (illustrative — TBD the DPS cooldowns before trusting the numbers):

  • Skulk vs rifle-marine-L0: dps ~ (75 bite / ~1.1 s) ≈ 68; ehp 95; → TI ≈ √(68 × 95/ (marine dps×ehp)) … normalizing to marine=1 → a skulk ≈ 1.2 TI (slightly above a rifle marine — matches the “2.1 hits to kill a marine, marine out-dps slowly” feel).
  • Lerk vs L0: dps ~ (60+poison)/~1 s ≈ 80; ehp 240; reach 1.2 (flight) → TI ≈ 2.6.
  • “Skulk + Lerk, both carapace” ≈ 1.2 + 2.6 (+carapace ehp bump) ≈ ~4.2 TI → that composition is a wave aimed at a 2-3-marine L1 team. This is the arithmetic the user wants; it needs the cooldown calibration and the carapace EHP delta to be real, not illustrative.

5.5 What we should decide together next

  1. Reference state per wave. Power is only meaningful vs a named marine loadout — do we compute the expected marine state from the economy model (start 60 + Σ payout + extractor income → what they can have bought by wave N), which we largely have from DESIGN §4?
  2. The cooldown gap. Alien DPS is animation-bound and not in Lua. Options: (a) measure TTK empirically from the chair / a scripted duel in Decoda; (b) accept per-hit × assumed-rate and calibrate later. Which?
  3. Sum or Lanchester. Do 15 skulks beat 1 onos? A flat sum says yes; square-law says the onos holds up better than that. Pick the aggregation.
  4. Utility/CC pricing. Stomp disrupt, gas, camouflage, web — force multipliers with no clean stat. Weight them, or fold into the reach/sustain multipliers?
  5. Where the horde’s knobs live. If waves are sized in TI, then the config ladder (Composition size, biomass gate, type mix) should be expressed as TI targets, and RD3 becomes “pick TI(wave), let the solver pick composition.” That reframes the whole balance task — worth a decision.
